## Problem Statement

In our communities, many individuals face challenges that leave them homebound, whether due to aging, chronic illness, or disability. Homebound living often leads to isolation, dependency, and difficulty completing everyday tasks. This project addresses the barriers faced by homebound individuals and aims to improve their quality of life.

---

## Summary

### Background

New York State has a population of approximately 20 million, with New York City accounting for over 8 million residents. According to the CDC (2022), approximately 27% of New York State residents have disabilities, and 41.1% of aging adults experience conditions that limit their activities.  

Key statistics include:
- **20%** of aging adults have mobility limitations.
- **12%** have difficulty with concentration, memory, or decision-making.
- **7%** struggle with running errands.
- **6%** experience vision problems.
- **3%** need help with daily self-care tasks like dressing or bathing.

The effects of disability and isolation often exacerbate mental health issues, such as depression and anxiety, which can lead to further physical and emotional health declines. Studies show that social connections play a critical role in reducing stress and improving health outcomes, but homebound individuals frequently lack these connections.  

Furthermore, while technology offers a potential solution for connection, reliance on digital platforms can lead to increased feelings of loneliness and mental health challenges. The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted these issues, showing the limitations of virtual interactions in creating meaningful connections.

## Solutions

### Existing Services

Several services exist to support homebound individuals, but they often come with significant limitations:

- **Delivery Services:**
  - **DoorDash:** High fees, subscription costs, and privacy concerns.
  - **Grubhub:** Similar issues with fees and deceptive advertising.
  - **UberEats:** Additional fees and controversies related to overcharging.
  - **Instacart:** Ethical concerns, fee structures, and pricing discrimination.
  - **Walmart+:** Affordable but geographically limited in New York City.

- **Nonprofit Services:**
  - **Meals on Wheels:** Provides meal delivery for seniors but does not address grocery delivery or support younger disabled populations.

- **Good Deeds Apps:**
  - **GetBack:** A broad-scope platform for community aid, but with significant usability and security issues.

### Proposed Solution

Benevolo seeks to address the gaps in existing solutions by providing a platform specifically tailored to homebound individuals. Our goals include:
- Offering budget-friendly delivery options with minimal fees.
- Ensuring accessibility for diverse age groups and physical abilities.
- Prioritizing user security and transparency in operations.
- Building a strong community-focused network to promote meaningful connections.

---

## Hypothesis

If users are able to request services from others in their local area for tasks they are unable to complete themselves, it will improve their quality of life and foster a stronger sense of community.

---

## Product Overview

Users would use the application whenever they wish to help somebody out. The app’s main focus is to do a good deed every day. Additionally, it caters to individuals who lack the ability to independently go out and run errands. This could also be incredibly beneficial during the summer and winter, given the extreme temperatures. The neighbor, acting as the task assigner, will upload the desired task for completion. The helper, acting as the task completer, will then choose a task based on their preference of what the task might entail, possibly depending on the location, and complete the task.

---

## Mission

Benevolo offers a platform where users can easily assist others in their community by completing daily tasks and errands, promoting acts of kindness and support for those who are unable to do so themselves. By connecting neighbors in need with willing helpers, Benevolo fosters a culture of goodwill and mutual aid, making it simple to perform a charitable deed daily while ensuring everyone has access to help when needed.

---

## Who We Serve

We serve people with disabilities or elderly people who need a helping hand. We also serve people who just don’t have the resources to do certain tasks, such as single parents. Our product can assist these individuals by enabling those in the community who are kind-hearted to assist them with their tasks.

## Research & Statistics

### Key Facts
- **DoorDash**: 61,126 users in NYS; majority earn <$25,000 annually.
- **Grubhub**: 60,793 users in NYS; similar demographic to DoorDash.
- **UberEats**: 25,072 NYC users; 36.7% earn <$25,000 annually.
- **Instacart**: Millions of users but with hidden fees and ethical concerns.
- **Meals on Wheels**: Delivered 16.7 million meals to NYS seniors in 2024.
- **Walmart+**: Free delivery for $98/year but limited access in NYC.

### Challenges with Existing Solutions
- High fees make most services unaffordable for low-income users.
- Limited coverage for non-senior homebound individuals.
- Issues with privacy, security, and usability in some platforms.
